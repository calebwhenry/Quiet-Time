# Quiet Time reading plan: NIV 1984 in a year

November 25, 2026 to November 24, 2027: 365 days, 1,189 chapters (about 3.3 a day). The Old Testament runs in canonical order all year; each New Testament book is read whole, alongside the Old Testament section it illuminates.

Daily loads are estimated from KJV word counts; the NIV runs a little shorter. Splits fall on chapter boundaries.

## Why each New Testament book is placed where it is

### Genesis with John, Galatians
*Days 1-28 (Nov 25 to Dec 22)*

John opens with 'In the beginning' and presents Jesus as the Word through whom all things were made, the Lamb God provides, Jacob's ladder (Jn 1:51) and the one who existed before Abraham (Jn 8:58). Galatians follows as the patriarchal narratives unfold: the promise to Abraham and his Seed (Gal 3), justification by faith (Gen 15:6), and Hagar and Sarah (Gal 4).

### Exodus with Mark
*Days 29-50 (Dec 23 to Jan 13)*

Mark is a gospel of the new exodus: it opens by quoting Exod 23:20 with Isaiah 40, and moves through wilderness, sea crossings, bread in the wilderness, a transfiguration on a mountain, and Passover with 'my blood of the covenant' (Mk 14:24; Exod 24:8).

### Leviticus with Hebrews
*Days 51-65 (Jan 14 to Jan 28)*

Hebrews is the New Testament's commentary on Leviticus: priesthood, sacrifices, the tabernacle, and the Day of Atonement (Lev 16; Heb 9-10) fulfilled in Christ the great high priest.

### Numbers with 1 Corinthians, 2 Peter, Jude
*Days 66-86 (Jan 29 to Feb 18)*

1 Corinthians 10 reads the wilderness events of Numbers (manna, the rock, grumbling, serpents, plague) as warnings 'written down for us'. 2 Peter and Jude then arrive as Numbers reaches Korah and Balaam (Num 16; 22-24; 2 Pet 2:15-16; Jude 11).

### Deuteronomy with Matthew
*Days 87-110 (Feb 19 to Mar 14)*

Jesus answers each temptation from Deuteronomy (Deut 6-8; Mt 4). Matthew presents him as the prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15) who teaches on the mountain and gathers his teaching into five great discourses.

### Joshua with Ephesians
*Days 111-120 (Mar 15 to Mar 24)*

Joshua's conquest and inheritance of the land pairs with Ephesians' inheritance in Christ (Eph 1:11-18) and the spiritual warfare of the armor of God (Eph 6).

### Judges to 2 Samuel with Luke
*Days 121-163 (Mar 25 to May 6)*

Luke's birth narratives deliberately echo 1 Samuel: Mary's song echoes Hannah's (1 Sam 2; Lk 1:46-55) and Jesus 'grew in favor with God and men' like Samuel (1 Sam 2:26; Lk 2:52). Bethlehem (Ruth) and the throne of David (2 Sam 7; Lk 1:32-33) run through it.

### 1 Kings to 2 Kings with Acts
*Days 164-196 (May 7 to Jun 8)*

Acts mirrors the Elijah-Elisha cycle: the master is taken up into heaven and his Spirit rests on his successors (2 Kgs 2; Acts 1-2), who then raise the dead and heal as he did. Kings tells of a kingdom divided and exiled; Acts of the kingdom going out to the nations.

### 1 Chronicles to 2 Chronicles with 1 Timothy, Titus, 2 Timothy
*Days 197-220 (Jun 9 to Jul 2)*

Chronicles orders worship, priests and Levites in God's house; the Pastoral Epistles order elders, deacons and worship in 'God's household' (1 Tim 3:15). 2 Timothy, Paul's farewell, arrives as Chronicles ends in exile with hope of return.

### Ezra to Esther with 1 Peter, Philemon
*Days 221-232 (Jul 3 to Jul 14)*

1 Peter addresses 'God's elect, strangers in the world' being built into a spiritual house (1 Pet 1:1; 2:5) while Ezra and Nehemiah rebuild the temple and walls among returned exiles. Philemon's intercession for Onesimus accompanies Esther's intercession for her people.

### Job with James
*Days 233-241 (Jul 15 to Jul 23)*

James explicitly points to 'Job's perseverance' (Jas 5:11) and addresses trials, testing and the wisdom that comes from God.

### Psalms with Philippians
*Days 242-262 (Jul 24 to Aug 13)*

Philippians is Paul's letter of rejoicing, written in chains, and carries a hymn of Christ's humiliation and exaltation (Phil 2) that reads naturally beside the lament and praise of the Psalms.

### Proverbs to Ecclesiastes with Colossians
*Days 263-272 (Aug 14 to Aug 23)*

Colossians presents Christ as the wisdom of God: 'all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge' (Col 2:3; Prov 2:4) and the one in whom all things were created (Col 1:15-17; Prov 8:22-31), against empty human philosophy.

### Song of Songs with 1 John, 2 John, 3 John
*Days 273-275 (Aug 24 to Aug 26)*

John's letters on love ('God is love', 1 Jn 4) accompany the Song's celebration of love; 2 John is addressed to 'the chosen lady'.

### Isaiah with 1 Thessalonians, 2 Thessalonians, Romans
*Days 276-298 (Aug 27 to Sep 18)*

Thessalonians draws its Day-of-the-Lord language from early Isaiah (2 Thess 1:9 / Isa 2:10-21; 2 Thess 2:8 / Isa 11:4). Romans follows for Isaiah's later chapters: it quotes Isaiah more than any other book (the remnant, 'how beautiful are the feet', the Servant, the Gentiles' hope; Rom 9-11, 15).

### Jeremiah to Lamentations with 2 Corinthians
*Days 299-322 (Sep 19 to Oct 12)*

2 Corinthians is Paul's defense of the new covenant written on hearts (2 Cor 3; Jer 31:31-34); he quotes 'let him who boasts boast in the Lord' (2 Cor 10:17; Jer 9:24) and, like Jeremiah, ministers through suffering and rejection.

### Ezekiel to Malachi with Revelation
*Days 323-365 (Oct 13 to Nov 24)*

Revelation draws on Ezekiel (throne vision, eating the scroll, Gog and Magog, the measured temple and river of life), Daniel (the beasts and the Son of Man) and Zechariah (horsemen, lampstands and olive trees, the one who was pierced). It closes the year with new creation as the OT closes with Malachi.
